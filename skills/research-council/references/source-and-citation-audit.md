# Source and citation audit

Read when collecting evidence and before reviewing sourced paragraphs. Inspect the exact version used; a newer revision, press release, abstract, and underlying study are distinct records.

## Record access and reading depth

For each source record title, author/organization, date/revision, URL or authorized file, and actual access depth: `full text inspected`, `relevant body sections inspected`, `abstract/opening summary only`, or `inaccessible`. Downloading a file is not reading it. Do not claim complete reading from selected pages.

For substantive source analysis, inspect the body passages supporting its question, population/period, data, methods, results, limitations, and contribution, as applicable to source type. Include relevant table/figure notes. Explain the evidence beneath headline findings rather than padding a summary with arbitrary detail counts. Abstracts may provide labeled exploratory leads; they do not establish uninspected methods, results, or limitations. When required source depth is unavailable, find an appropriate accessible source, narrow the claim, or mark the gap unverified.

## Trace claims precisely

Maintain a claim ledger for substantive borrowed claims, statistics, quotations, and method descriptions. Record claim ID and paragraph/version; quote/paraphrase/inference status; exact source/version; checked original passage and locator; population, period, units and denominator; intended citation; limitations; and verification result. Routine common knowledge does not require artificial citations.

Record printed page numbers separately from PDF-viewer positions. Cite real locators according to the requested style. Do not invent pages, sections, paragraph numbers, or timestamps for unpaginated material. Verify statistics against original values, labels, notes, and denominators. Evidence supporting a narrow statement does not support every broader claim in its paragraph.

## Audit quotation and paraphrase separately

Check direct quotations against the inspected original. Preserve meaning, mark quotation boundaries, attribute at point of use, supply real locators where available, and identify omissions/alterations correctly.

Write paraphrases from understanding, then compare wording and sentence structure with the original. Synonym substitution is insufficient. Quote distinctive borrowed phrasing or rewrite substantially, and cite the underlying idea. A bibliography entry does not replace in-text attribution; an end-of-paragraph citation must not leave source boundaries ambiguous.

Check that each citation resolves to the correct bibliography record and the version actually inspected. Phrase-matching tools may identify passages to inspect but cannot certify original paraphrasing or research validity. Never invent quotations, source access, findings, or citation metadata.
