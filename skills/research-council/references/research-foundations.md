# Research foundations

Read during context gathering and outline review; revisit when new evidence changes the question or argument. Scale records to the task while keeping consequential uncertainties visible.

## Define the question and measures

Preserve the user's current question, distinguishing it from historical or proposed wording. Define substantive or ambiguous terms before relying on them. Separate conceptual definitions from operational measures. Specify population, geography, period, units, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and comparison groups where relevant. Justify proxies, thresholds, and category boundaries; convenient choices are not established facts.

## Make assumptions and inference explicit

For each consequential assumption, record its ID, statement, justification, evidence status, affected claims, consequence if false, and a feasible sensitivity check or missing evidence. Include measurement, sampling, modeling, and causal assumptions where applicable. Do not present assumptions as observations.

Map the reasoning: research question → definitions → premises → evidence → inference → conclusion. Check consistency, circular reasoning, counterexamples, alternative explanations, selection effects, and whether the inference exceeds the design. A proposed mechanism is not an established cause; an association is not automatically a causal effect. Absence of a clear difference is not proof of no effect.

## Synthesize compatible evidence

Before combining findings, compare source populations, periods, definitions, measures, denominators, methods, and source versions. Identify which differences permit comparison and which prevent pooling. Related outcomes are not interchangeable: a process benefit does not automatically establish an economic outcome, and one time horizon does not establish another.

Multiple reports from the same dataset do not become independent studies. Distinguish corroboration from repeated reporting of one finding. Explain what each source contributes, what challenges the interpretation, and what remains unanswered. Calibrate confidence to source support, not persona agreement. A defensible conclusion may narrow or reject the original premise.
